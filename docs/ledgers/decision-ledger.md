Because `run_zone_fill_authority` must read the cache before it can compare it to a refill, those seven were refused with a *resource* diagnostic before freshness was ever considered; at 200,000 the split moves from 9 `fresh` / 2 `stale_fill` / 7 refused to **12 `fresh` / 6 `stale_fill` / 0 refused**. **The finding that decides the design is that this budget sits behind the parse it looks like it prevents.** `read_fill_islands` parses the whole document before counting a vertex, so refusing the largest corpus board at a budget of 3 costs 20.9 s of the 24.2 s a complete read costs -- 86 % of its own workload is unpreventable -- and what it actually meters is 22-26 us and 113-145 B per admitted vertex. The defence against an unbounded vertex list is `ParseLimits` ([ADR-0079](../adr/0079-discriminated-configurable-parse-budgets.md)), which also caps the population: no document can offer more than **741,375** fill vertices at the shipped parse defaults before `budget.exceeded.nodes` refuses it. The new number follows ADR-0079's rule at the densest observation -- 16 MiB x 29,503 vertices/MiB = 472,048, rounded up -- and is **robust to the private corpus**, because the densest in-repository board gives 435,824 and rounds to the same 500,000. The range stays 3-1,000,000: the ceiling is still reachable (a 741,375-vertex document reads at it) and is deliberately left below the 2,097,152 the fixed 16 MiB byte ceiling would permit. Priced rather than asserted: against the densest reachable document the change moves a refusal from 29.325 s / 146.8 MiB to 35.812 s / 168.4 MiB, so it **buys an attacker 6.5 s and 21.6 MiB** (+22 % / +15 %) against a floor that is the parse; `COPPER_MCP_MAX_FILL_VERTICES=50000` restores the old posture exactly. **One budget does meter two populations and that is deliberately *not* answered with a second knob**, which is where this departs from [ADR-0089](../adr/0089-region-scoped-obstacle-model.md): it is charged once on the operator's cached pour and once on KiCad's recomputation, and the committed `zone-fill-stale` fixture caches 148 vertices while KiCad recomputes 186, so every budget in [148, 185] admits the board and then refuses this server's own output. But across six stale corpus boards the two differ by at most 0.03 % (-24 to +2 vertices), where #128's populations were three orders of magnitude apart -- there is no second distribution to fit a second number to. What that finding does justify is the diagnostic fix it exposed: `_points` hardcoded "cached", so the refilled call site produced the self-contradicting `refilled zone fill could not be read: cached zone fill exceeds the configured vertex budget`; the reader no longer guesses, because both call sites already say which document they hold. **Raising it is answer-preserving, proved from the budget and not from a differential**: `max_vertices` reaches exactly one expression -- the guard that aborts `_points` -- so it decides whether a read happens and never what it returns; the threshold is exactly the board's vertex *total* (never a per-island one, which `zone-fill-islands` separates at 186 across two islands whose larger is 94), and above it the islands, the `fill_digest` and `ZoneFillAuthority.to_dict()` are identical from the vertex count to the ceiling while KiCad recomputes the refill each time. No content address moves and no version constant advances, because this budget bounds what is admitted and never what is written. **No route is unlocked and none is claimed** -- B-105 measured that at zero, and only one converting board has an `F.Cu` zone at all. One refusal moves rather than disappearing, which is the expected outcome of raising a gate: this budget meters a board's total pour while every consumer charges the widest island, and 14 of 18 corpus boards carry an island above the ordered-layer adapter's 4,096 ceiling (widest 43,889), so boards that could not be read at all will now read and then be refused per-island.
