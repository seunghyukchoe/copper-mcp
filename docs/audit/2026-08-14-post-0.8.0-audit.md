# Post-0.8.0 audit and improvement plan

A dated audit of the repository at `0bc72da`, the commit that authorized the `v0.8.0` release. It
covers four dimensions — correctness debt, evidence integrity, public contracts, and the process
that produced both — and ends in an ordered improvement plan whose every item names the check that
proves it done.

This is a **dated snapshot**, not a living document. Where it and the repository disagree, the
repository is right, and the correction belongs in a later record rather than in an edit here. The
one thing it does own going forward is the plan's item numbering (`P0.1` … `P7.2`), which the
tracker comments filed on 2026-08-14 cite by name.

|---|---|
| Date | 2026-08-14 |
| Commit audited | `0bc72da` (`v0.8.0` authorization), on `main` |
| Method | Two independent passes — an evidence/correctness pass and a roadmap pass — arbitrated into one deliverable. No code changed. |

## 0. How to read this document

### Verification labels

Every load-bearing claim below carries one of three labels, and the labels are not decoration. This
project has published claims that rested on measurements nobody could re-run, and the whole of
section 2 is about that; a document diagnosing it that did not itself distinguish "I ran this" from
"I read this" would be making the same mistake in a smaller font.

| Label | Means |
|---|---|
| **verified** | A command was run against the audited tree and its output read. |
| **read-but-not-verified** (`rbnv`) | Taken from a record — a ledger row, an ADR, an issue — and not independently re-derived. |
| **not_run** | Named because its absence matters. Nothing was executed and nothing is claimed. |

### The estimate discipline

Estimates are in **delivered agent-hours**, calibrated as one agent run ≈ 44 min × 1.4 rework ≈ 1.0
delivered agent-hour, against an observed 13 merges in the week since `v0.7.0`.

**Nine plan items carry no estimate at all.** Each of them rests on a premise nobody has measured,
so each is split into a measure-first sub-item and the estimate is withheld until that sub-item
runs. Section 6 lists all nine.

> **The rule, stated once and applied throughout: converting a withheld item into a number without
> running its measure-first sub-item is the finding, not the fix.** A number produced that way
> describes the estimator's confidence, not the work, and it is exactly the move that turned a
> 50–80 hour figure into a milestone entry criterion nobody could evaluate (section 5, `M3`).

### The number discipline

Three phrases recur below and each one is load-bearing.

- **16 of 465.** `B-107`'s route-preview measurement over the private working corpus: 465 route
  verdicts, of which 16 `routed` and 324 `already_connected`, the rest typed refusals. No sentence
  in this document, or in the roadmap, may describe routing capability in terms that 16 of 465 does
  not support. (`rbnv` for the verdict breakdown; **verified** that the nine published verdict
  counts sum to 465.)
- **Legal-as-found.** `B-099`'s close-out replay ran placement preview **without rules**: 1,283
  verdicts, 1,127 previewed and 156 `refused/illegal_placement`. A clean verdict there means the
  board was legal as the designer left it — it is not a placement-quality result, and the 156
  refusals are 156 courtyard overlaps already in the board rather than 156 bad candidates.
- **13 of 18 saves.** The conversion figure is 13 of the 18 saves in the private working corpus,
  which hold 17 distinct board contents (one pair is byte-identical across two save directories), so
  the same result is 13 of 17 distinct boards. State which denominator a count uses. Five saves
  refuse: four on a custom-shape SMD pad and one on a root copper graphic.

## 1. Correctness ranking

**None of the four open correctness issues is in the forbidden class.** The forbidden class is a
silent under-approximation of an obstacle — copper the model does not know about, carried into a
candidate and then into an apply. Every one of the four is either an over-refusal, a bookkeeping gap
that fails loudly, or a decided-and-recorded refusal. That is the single most important sentence in
this audit, and it is why the plan below is ordered by contract exposure rather than by alarm.

Ranked by exposure, worst first.

### 1.1 — #164: layered fill-authority parity, and it diverges in both directions

[#164](https://github.com/seunghyukchoe/copper-mcp/issues/164) asks for a public layered
fill-authority contract and `routing_effect` provenance on `preview_layered_route`. The audit finds
it is not a documentation gap but a **parity divergence between the single-layer and layered
paths**, and the divergence runs in both directions.

**Verified**: `LayeredRouteCandidate`
(`src/copper_mcp/routing/layered_contracts.py`) records `candidate_id`, `base_revision`,
`start_pad_id`, `end_pad_id`, `patch`, `cost`, `metrics`, `settings`, `router_version`, `policy` and
`seed` — and no fill binding of any kind. The single-layer path gained exactly that binding in
0.8.0: [ADR-0103](../adr/0103-a-candidate-records-the-model-that-produced-it.md) made a candidate
record the fill model that produced it and refuse a replay under any other, with the
`fill_evidence_mismatch` diagnostic. On the layered path that invariant is simply unenforced.

Direction of error, both ways:

- **Under-recording.** A layered candidate produced under one fill model can be replayed under
  another with nothing to notice. This is the direction ADR-0103 exists to close.
- **Over-refusal.** The public layered seam never supplies `verified_fill` at all — **verified**:
  `src/copper_mcp/layered_route_preview.py` builds its `LayeredRouteRequest` without the field, so
  it takes the dataclass default of `()`. So today the layered path cannot reach fill evidence and
  the divergence is latent rather than live.

**Latent is not safe, because one of ADR-0103's two named triggers has already fired.**
ADR-0103 names two circumstances under which the layered gap would become live, and the vertex
budget raise is one of them — [ADR-0104](../adr/0104-fill-vertex-budget-behind-a-parse.md) landed it
in the same release, taking `max_fill_vertices` from 50,000 to 500,000. The gap is therefore one
flag away from mattering.

**Ordering constraint, and it is the blocking part of plan item P2.1:** the layered binding must
land **with or before** any public `include_fill_authority` on the layered seam. Shipping the flag
first would make the divergence reachable through the public surface for however long the binding
takes.

### 1.2 — #167: over-refusal, and the issue misstates its own fallback

[#167](https://github.com/seunghyukchoe/copper-mcp/issues/167) records that the ordered-layer
per-island fill ceiling of 4,096 vertices is reached by 14 of 18 corpus boards. The direction is
over-refusal, which is the safe direction — but the mechanism is not the one the issue describes,
and a fix written against the described mechanism would be written against a fallback that does not
exist.

The issue's third "what a fix would need" condition says: *"Refusing an island keeps the zone's
conservative outline envelope, so the current behaviour is refusal-side and safe."*

**Verified**, in `src/copper_mcp/routing/layered_board_adapter.py`: `_invalid_verified_fill` is
reached from `_invalid_request`, which `LayeredBoardRouter.propose` calls **before any snapshot
work**. An island above `_MAX_FILL_VERTICES` returns the string `verified fill island is not a
bounded polygon`, and `propose` converts that into a whole-request `INVALID_REQUEST` diagnostic.
There is no per-island skip and no degradation to the outline envelope. **One over-ceiling island
refuses the entire route request.**

The conclusion survives — the behaviour is refusal-side, and in fact *more* refusing than the issue
claims. What does not survive is the premise a fix would build on: the graceful per-island
degradation the issue describes would have to be **built**, not preserved.

The cost payable today lands elsewhere. **Verified**: the single-layer core has no per-island
ceiling; it walks a ring per pairwise test and charges `max_obstacle_checks`, so an over-wide island
surfaces there as `obstacle_check_budget_exceeded`. `B-105` measured that path's route benefit on
the corpus at zero — 2 connectivity improvements, 6 budget regressions, 0 routes unlocked (`rbnv`).

### 1.3 — #166: contract hygiene, and the dangerous direction is already gated

[#166](https://github.com/seunghyukchoe/copper-mcp/issues/166) reports that the single-layer
`verified_fill` seam performs no shape validation where the layered adapter validates all of it.

**Verified**: containment checking is present on **both** paths. What the single-layer seam is
missing is type and size validation of the incoming value, not the geometric containment test. The
failure mode of the gap is therefore a loud Python exception on a malformed input, not wrong copper
in a candidate. That is contract hygiene — worth closing, and correctly ranked below the two above.

This issue is deliberately left **unmilestoned**: it needs a decision before it needs a milestone
(plan item P7.2), and the decision may be to close it.

### 1.4 — #141: decided-with-exits, not permanent

The roadmap has described [#141](https://github.com/seunghyukchoe/copper-mcp/issues/141) — a root
copper graphic, `gr_text` on `F.Cu` — as "answered by decision and staying refused". That is right
about the answer and wrong about its permanence.

[ADR-0095](../adr/0095-copper-text-has-no-derivable-envelope.md) writes **five falsifiable exit
conditions**: four jointly required, plus a fifth that applies to `gr_text_box` alone and prevents
the two heads being split apart even if the first four were met. A decision with written exits is
not a permanent non-goal; it is a decision that can be reopened on evidence.

**Verified**: none of the five moved in 0.8.0. Condition 1 was checked directly — nothing in the
source digests `text_variables`; the token's three occurrences are all non-reading, and root board
properties convert as an unmodelled count
([ADR-0094](../adr/0094-root-board-properties-as-metadata.md), `R-139`) rather than as an expanded
map.

The arbitrated label is therefore: **"decided by ADR-0095; five exit conditions written, none met,
none in progress"** — and the issue was closed on 2026-08-14 carrying that label together with the
five conditions, so that reopening is mechanical rather than a re-reading of the ADR.

## 2. Evidence integrity

### 2.1 Mutation evidence: the standard landed, the backlog stays labelled

[ADR-0098](../adr/0098-reproducible-mutation-evidence.md) fixed the class. Mutation runs now go
through a committed harness that purges bytecode caches around every application and restoration,
refuses a mutant whose anchor does not match exactly once, and proves kills in both directions;
specs live under [`docs/mutants/`](../mutants/README.md) with a mutant-to-killing-test mapping.

**Verified at `0bc72da`**: 7 specs / 66 mutants, all dated 2026-08-13, anchor gate green.

The backlog is where the honesty has to hold. `R-143` classifies the 24 pre-ADR-0098 hand-applied
runs (~170 mutants, a lower bound): 2 `exposed`, 22 `unauditable`, and **none shown false**. The
audit re-derived exactly one of the 24 — 3 of ADR-0096's 23 — so 23 remain unauditable in fact and
not merely in classification.

The direction of error is the optimistic one and it is worth stating plainly: a stale mutant
poisoning the next invocation registers as a *false kill*, so "0 survivors" is the flattering side
of the defect. The standing rule from `R-143` — a record citing a pre-ADR-0098 mutation claim must
cite it with the `unauditable`/`exposed` literal rather than as a settled kill count — is not
mechanically enforced and remains reviewer-owned.

### 2.2 #170: KiCad DRC counts are not reproducible, and nothing in the ledgers says so yet

[#170](https://github.com/seunghyukchoe/copper-mcp/issues/170) was filed out of `B-107`, which found
that two baseline runs at the *same commit* on *identical bytes* still differed on nine boards —
every one of them only in the `drc` section. `error_count` moved 936 → 941 on one board;
`hole_clearance` moved 201 → 202; a whole violation type (`tracks_crossing`, 4) appeared in one run
and not the other.

**Verified**: no risk row and no benchmark correction row exists for this. The exposure is prose
only — bare DRC counts are published in `B-099`, `B-103` and the tier-2 capability note, with
nothing beside them saying the counts are one invocation's answer rather than the board's.
**Verified**: nothing in the repository is a `drc-summary` schema instance, so no committed artifact
carries the unqualified claim.

`B-108` corroborates the mechanism (`rbnv`): the counts **saturate near per-rule caps** —
199/200/201, 499/500/502 — and which rules fill the caps varies run to run. Baseline-against-baseline
disagreement reaches |Δ| 32 against 109 across a change: a factor of 3.4, not a factor of 100. So
the noise does not swamp a real signal, but it is the same order of magnitude as one, which is
precisely why a differential quoting a single invocation is unsafe.

**The policy is decided and is not a tolerance.** A numeric tolerance would be an oracle-fitted
constant, and ADR-0095 already refused that shape of argument in another domain: *an envelope is
derived and then oracle-checked; an oracle-fitted constant inverts that.* The policy instead is a
required one-value comparability literal on the DRC section —
`single_invocation | repeated_agreement | repeated_disagreement` — with the prohibition that **no
differential may cite a count that is not `repeated_agreement`**. Existing rows are qualified by a
correction row in the benchmark ledger following the `B-102` pattern, not retracted. Plan items
P4.1 and P4.1a.

### 2.3 The corpus figures at `0bc72da` are argued-forward

This is the label the arbitration attached, and it is carried everywhere the figures appear.

**Argued-forward** means: the figure was measured on a tree that is not the audited tree, and the
argument that it still holds is a reasoning step rather than a measurement. Specifically:

- `B-107`'s baseline is `f0f7414`, which **predates** ADR-0104 — **verified** by ancestor check.
- **Verified**: `max_fill_vertices` reaches exactly two call sites in `run_zone_fill_authority`, so
  the raise's blast radius is bounded and enumerable.
- **Verified** (upgraded from the findings' `rbnv`): `scripts/benchmark_real_board_capability.py`,
  the corpus runner behind `B-107` and `B-103`, never sets `include_fill_authority`. The flag
  appears nowhere in that file; the only script that sets it is
  `scripts/benchmark_fill_preview_provenance.py`, which supplies its islands directly.
- **not_run**: no harness run exists on a tree carrying both changes.

So the corpus numbers this document quotes — 13 of 18, 16 of 465, 1,127 of 1,283, 5 of 13 appliable
— are the best available and are **not** measurements of `0bc72da`. Plan item P1.1a is the
conversion of the argument into a measurement, and until it runs, every one of these figures keeps
this label.

A second reason the label cannot be dropped by waiting: `R-146` records that the corpus is one
designer's **live** working tree, edited during long runs. `B-107` recorded a phono board changing
between run 1 and run 2 and again between run 2 and run 3, and restricted every comparison to the 17
saves whose bytes were identical. A figure from that tree is only true of the corpus state that
produced it. That is what P1.1's frozen redistributable corpus exists to end.

## 3. Contracts

### 3.1 Schema drift: four instances, three releases, two directions

This is the finding that promotes [#172](https://github.com/seunghyukchoe/copper-mcp/issues/172)
from hygiene to an M1 gate. #172 reports that `schemas/board-ir/0.2.0.schema.json` was widened in
place — the thing [ADR-0096](../adr/0096-edge-connector-pads-convert-as-smd.md) declined to do. The
audit swept every schema across every released tag and found the practice is neither new nor
confined to Board IR.

**Verified** by comparing each schema's blob at all eight tags:

| Release | Schema | Change | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| `v0.3.0` | `audio-benchmark-catalog/0.1.0` | `+expected_pad_count` as a **required** key; `+multi-pin-route-preview` enum member | **narrowing** (plus a widening) |
| `v0.7.0` | `board-ir/0.2.0` | `+courtyard_circles` and its `courtyard_circle` `$def`; `net_id` widened to accept `null` on via, segment and arc | widening |
| `v0.7.0` | `drc-summary` | `+clean` as a **required** key, with an `allOf` pinning both derivations | **narrowing** |
| `v0.8.0` | `board-ir/0.2.0` | `+far_side_courtyards`, `+far_side_courtyard_circles` — #172's instance | widening |

Controls, also **verified**: `board-ir/0.1.0` is byte-identical at every tag from `v0.2.0` (where it
first appears) through `v0.8.0`; `circuit-intent/0.1.0`, `circuit-schematic-build/0.1.0`,
`candidate` and `board-manifest` never change after introduction. So the drift is a property of four
specific files, not of the repository's habits generally.

Two consequences the single-instance framing misses:

1. **Two of the four break in the direction #172 does not discuss.** A required-key addition is a
   *narrowing*: a document that validated yesterday fails today. #172 argues about widening, which
   is the forgiving direction. The gate this audit specifies therefore has to fire on both.
2. **A `0.2.0` document could have been produced under three different accepted sets** spanning
   `v0.5.0`–`v0.8.0`, and they are not interchangeable. **Verified**: the `footprint` `$def` carries
   `additionalProperties: false`, so a document produced at `v0.8.0` carrying `far_side_courtyards`
   is *rejected* by the `0.2.0` schema as published at `v0.5.0`. One version string, three accepted
   sets, one-directional incompatibility.

**The decision is pre-arbitrated** (plan item P3.1): bump to `0.3.0`, freeze `0.2.0` as published, no
retroactive correction, and a migration note stating plainly that `0.2.0`-as-published spans three
accepted sets. The ADR must cover `drc-summary` and `audio-benchmark-catalog` as well — a Board
IR-only decision fixes one of three affected files. And #172's thesis is restated rather than
adopted: **ADR-0096 was the first articulation of the rule, not the rule itself**, so the three
instances that predate #172's framing are instances of the same class and not exceptions to it.

### 3.2 In-process counters invisible to MCP clients: five, not three

The brief carried this as three counters. The audit found more.

**Verified**, `src/copper_mcp/board_ir/diagnostics.py`: `ConversionResult` carries **five** measured
fields — `max_roundrect_rounding_nm`, `unmodelled_group_count`, `edge_connector_pad_count`,
`unmodelled_board_property_count` and `unmodelled_pad_property_count`. **Verified**:
`BoardIrSummary` (`src/copper_mcp/board_ir_service.py`) carries none of them.

Four of the five disclose an *erasure* that a documented risk depends on: `R-134` (groups), `R-139`
(board properties), `R-141` (edge-connector pads) and `R-144` (pad fabrication properties) each say,
in effect, "the conversion loses the token, and this count is the disclosure". If the count never
reaches an MCP client, the disclosure does not happen and the mitigation those four rows record is
partial. **Direction of error: under-disclosure.**

The fix shape is deliberately one field rather than five: `BoardIrSummary` gains a single
`unmodelled_counts` map, and the proving check is a contract test asserting that **every measured
field on `ConversionResult` appears in the map**. That way a sixth counter becomes an entry rather
than a contract change — which is the whole reason this grew from three to five without anyone
noticing. Plan item P3.6.

### 3.3 Deleted refusal messages: a golden set, not a deprecation path

Three refusal messages disappeared from the source entirely in 0.8.0. The migration note is complete
and the change is correct; the question is what stops the next one being silent.

**Deprecation is the wrong answer here.** A deprecation path would have the server emit a message it
no longer means — false output, to protect a consumer of a string. **A rule is also the wrong
answer**, because a rule was already the regime and it held by luck rather than by construction.

The right answer is a **golden set**: the emitted refusal messages are already closed tables in one
file, so a test can pin the set and fail when a member leaves it. **Verified** in
`src/copper_mcp/adapters/kicad_board_ir.py`: `_UNMODELLED_ROOT_HEADS`, `_COPPER_TEXT_REFUSAL` /
`_UNNAMED_COPPER_GRAPHIC`, and `_UNMODELLED_COPPER_GRAPHIC_HEADS` are exactly such closed tables,
and the file's own comment records why the message is *a value from the table, selected by equality
against the source token and never built from it* — so pinning the table pins the contract without
pinning board bytes. Plan item P3.7.

## 4. Process verdicts

The question for each failure class this session produced: **does it get a checker, or a rule?** The
test applied is narrow on purpose — a checker is warranted when detection is *local, offline and
exact*. Everything else is a rule, and a rule is honest about being reviewer-owned rather than
pretending to be a gate.

| Class | Instances | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Schema-set drift | 4, **verified** across all 8 tags | **Checker.** A ~60-line probe already exists as a seed implementation. |
| Commit-message check has no server twin | **verified** client-only | **Wire the existing checker into CI.** The class bit the main loop twice. |
| CI-budget drift; two `make lint` checkers never in CI | **verified**: `ci.yml` sets no `timeout-minutes`; the audio-benchmark and circuit-intent checkers never run there | **Checker** (budget) **+ wire** (the two). |
| Refusal-message deletion | 3 this release | **Golden set** (section 3.3). Not a deprecation path. |
| Code-symbol citations | 1 (an ADR-0095 fix); **verified** 185 bare citations against 8 tag-pinned | **Rule + a format check on new citations only.** Truth stays reviewer-owned. |
| Stale poll-loops | 3 forms, **no artifact** | **Rule only.** No gate is possible — there is nothing to inspect. |

The last row is the one worth generalising, and it generalises ADR-0098's own lesson beyond mutation
testing: **an absence is evidence only if the observation was capable of reporting a presence.** A
poll loop that never had a channel to the thing it polled reports "nothing happened" identically to
one that watched successfully. Plan item P6.5 puts that sentence in the agent contract, where it
covers a class no checker can reach.

## 8. Corrections this audit made to its own inputs

Two factual errors were found in the findings the audit was executing. Both are corrected here and
the corrected version is what the plan carries.

### 8.1 The `diagnostics.py` docstring drift is in a different sentence

The finding recorded the drift as the docstring saying "the four above". **Verified**: that phrase
is in `unmodelled_pad_property_count`'s paragraph — the fifth counter — where **four** counters do
precede it, so it is correct.

The actual drift is one paragraph earlier. `unmodelled_board_property_count` says *"It is a count
for the same reason the **two fields above** are"*, and **three** counters precede it. The paragraph
was written when only the rounding and group counters existed; `edge_connector_pad_count`'s
paragraph (ADR-0096) was later inserted **above** it without the sentence being updated — visible in
the source as a missing blank line between the two paragraphs.

This does not change P3.6's shape or estimate. It changes which sentence P3.6 fixes, and it is a
small instance of the class section 4 is about: a hand-maintained cross-reference with no mechanical
check, drifting exactly where a new item was inserted.

### 8.2 `board-ir/0.1.0` is a control from `v0.2.0`, not from `v0.1.0`

The finding described `board-ir/0.1.0` as "stable at every tag". **Verified**: the file does not
exist at `v0.1.0`; it first appears at `v0.2.0` and is byte-identical from there through `v0.8.0`.
It is a valid control — arguably a stronger one, since it spans the same releases in which the
`0.2.0` schema drifted three times — but the claim as written overstates its span by one tag.

### 8.3 Two things this audit deliberately did **not** correct

- **The `≈23h` arithmetic** (section 5, 0.9.0 scope). The itemised numbers do not sum to it. Rather
  than adjust either the total or the items to agree, both are published with the gap named. The
  arbitrated total is the decision; the itemised figures are the evidence; and four items carry a
  bound rather than an estimate.
- **`#65`'s "CC0"** description of PCBWorld/PCBench. It is wrong — PCBench is MIT and PCBWorld is
  CC-BY-4.0-plus-heterogeneous, corrected in the open-baseline benchmarks research note — but
  correcting an issue **body** was outside this execution's authorization. It stays as plan item
  P2.2.
